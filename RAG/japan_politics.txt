Q: What type of political system does Japan have?
A: Japan operates as a dominant-party bicameral parliamentary representative democratic constitutional monarchy. A hereditary monarch serves as head of state, while an elected Prime Minister serves as head of government. Sovereignty is vested in the people of Japan under the 1947 Constitution.

Q: Who is the current Emperor of Japan?
A: The emperor of Japan is currently Naruhito since May 1 2019. The 126th monarch.

Q: Who is currently the Prime Minister of Japan?
A: Sanae Takaichi has served as Prime Minister since October 21, 2025. She is notable for being the first woman to serve as President of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) and the first woman to serve as Prime Minister.

Q: What is the role of the Emperor in Japan?
A: The Emperor of Japan serves as a symbolic figure and does not have political power. Article 1 of the Constitution of Japan defines the Emperor to be "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the people". The role is mainly ceremonial, such as appointing the Prime Minister and performing state functions. All actions of the Emperor must be approved by the government.

Q: What is the role of the Prime Minister in Japan?
A: The Prime Minister is the head of government and chief of the executive branch (the Cabinet). They are nominated by the National Diet and formally appointed by the Emperor. The Prime Minister has power to appoint and dismiss Cabinet ministers, dissolve the lower house of the Diet, and wields significant informal power through party support, public popularity, and influence over the bureaucracy.

Q: Who holds political power in Japan?
A: Political power is held mainly by the Prime Minister, Cabinet, and other elected members of the National Diet.

Q: Who can succeed the Emperor?
A: The Imperial Throne is succeeded by a paternal male member of the Imperial House as designated by the Imperial Household Law.

Q: What is the National Diet of Japan?
A: The National Diet (Kokkai) is Japan's national legislature and the highest organ of state power. It is the sole law-making body of the state. It is responsible for making laws, approving the budget, and nominating the Prime Minister. It is bicameral, consisting of the House of Representatives (lower house) and the House of Councillors (upper house), and meets at the National Diet Building.

Q: How is the National Diet chosen?
A: Both houses of the National Diet are directly elected under a parallel voting system.

Q: How is the National Diet structured?
A: The National Diet has two houses: the House of Representatives and the House of Councillors. The House of Representatives has eighteen standing committees ranging from 20 to 50 members, while the House of Councillors has sixteen committees ranging from 10 to 45 members. Both houses must pass legislation in identical form for it to become law.

Q: What does the House of Representatives do?
A: The House of Representatives is the lower and more powerful house of the National Diet. It can force the resignation of the government, has ultimate control over the passage of the budget, ratification of treaties, and the selection of the Prime Minister. It can also override decisions made by the House of Councillors. Members are elected for four-year terms, though the Prime Minister's power to dissolve the house means members often serve less than four years.

Q: What does the House of Councillors do?
A: The upper house, the House of Councillors, is very weak and bills are sent to the House of Councillors only to be approved, not made. Members of the upper house are elected for six-year terms with half the members elected every three years.

Q: Is it possible for different parties to control the National Diet?
A: It is possible for different parties to control the lower house and the upper house, a situation referred to as a "twisted Diet".

Q: How is the Prime Minister of Japan selected?
A: The Prime Minister is selected by members of the National Diet. Usually, the leader of the majority party in the House of Representatives is chosen. The Emperor then formally appoints the Prime Minister.

Q: What role does the Constitution play in Japan's political system?
A: Japan's current Constitution was adopted in 1947. The Constitution of Japan defines the structure of government and limits political power. It establishes the roles of the Emperor, the Diet, and the Cabinet, and guarantees fundamental rights for citizens.

Q: How are laws made in Japan?
A: Laws in Japan are proposed in the National Diet and must be approved by both houses. If there is disagreement, the House of Representatives can override the House of Councillors in certain cases. Most legislation that is considered in the National Diet is proposed by the cabinet.

Q: What is the separation of powers in Japan?
A: Japan's political system separates power into three branches: the legislative branch (the National Diet), the executive branch (the Cabinet), and the judicial branch (the Supreme Court). This separation helps prevent any one part of government from becoming too powerful.

Q: How does the Cabinet of Japan work?
A: The Cabinet is led by the Prime Minister and holds executive power. Cabinet members are nominated by the Prime Minister and are required to be civilians.

Q: Which party is in power in Japan?

Q: Which party is the most dominant in Japan?
A: Japanese politics in the post-war period has largely been dominated by the ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP), which has been in power almost continuously since its foundation in 1955. Of the 32 prime ministers since the end of the country's occupation in 1952, 25 as well as the longest serving ones have been members of the LDP. Consequently, Japan has been described as a de facto one-party state.

Q: What are the key features of Japan's Constitution?
A: Its key features include: sovereignty of the people, democratic institutions, expanded suffrage to women, a parliamentary system, labor rights, and the peace clause.

Q: What does Article 9 of Japan's Constitution say?
A: Article 9 is the peace clause of Japan's Constitution. It renounces war and prohibits the threat or use of force as a means of settling international disputes.

It has been in power almost continuously since its founding. Q: What is the role of the Supreme Court in Japan?
A: The Supreme Court of Japan has ultimate judicial authority to interpret the Japanese Constitution and decide questions of national law. It has the power of judicial review, which allows it to determine the constitutionality of any law or official act.

Q: How does policy-making work in Japan?
A: Policy-making in Japan involves close collaboration among the ruling party, the elite bureaucracy, and important interest groups. Ideas are often developed informally among elite circles first, then formalized through deliberation councils. Real negotiations often take place privately through the nemawashi (root binding) process of consensus building, in which differences are worked out behind the scenes before any formal action is taken.
